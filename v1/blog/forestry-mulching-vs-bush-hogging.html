Forestry Mulching vs. Bush Hogging: Which Do You Need?

April 26, 2026

If you own a few acres in Northwest Florida, sooner or later you will be told you need either a bush hog or a forestry mulcher. They sound similar, they cost different amounts, and they produce very different results. Here's the plain-English breakdown.

What each one actually does

A bush hog is a heavy rotary mower pulled behind a tractor. The blades cut grass, weeds, and light brush — anything woody bigger than about 1" in diameter starts to choke it.

A forestry mulcher is a drum studded with carbide teeth, mounted on a tracked carrier. It grinds standing brush, palmetto, and small trees right down into mulch.

When a bush hog is the right answer

  • You already have an open field or pasture and you're maintaining it
  • Growth is mostly grass and light weeds, no woody stems
  • You're mowing on a routine schedule (annually or more)

When you need forestry mulching

  • Palmetto, gallberry, or yaupon understory
  • Any standing trees you need to clear
  • Briars, smilax, or vines
  • Land that hasn't been maintained for several years
  • Storm debris

The quick rule of thumb

If you're maintaining open land that's already been cleared once, bush hog it. If you're reclaiming land — taking back acreage that has grown up — mulch it. Once mulched, a bush hog is a great way to keep up with the regrowth on a routine schedule.

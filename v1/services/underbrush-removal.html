Underbrush Removal — Keep the Trees, Lose the Mess

Remove palmetto, briar, vines, and invasive understory while leaving your mature pines and hardwoods untouched.

A lot of Panhandle properties are beautiful under all that understory — owners just can't see it. Underbrush removal opens up the woods, dramatically reduces fire load near the home, and makes the land usable again, all without taking down a single tree you want to keep.

What's included

  • Removal of palmetto, gallberry, yaupon, smilax, and other understory
  • Careful work around mature pines, oaks, and hardwoods
  • Mulch layer left in place to suppress regrowth
  • Hand finish work around trunks and protected features

When this service makes sense

  • Improving views, walkability, and habitat
  • Reducing wildfire fuel load around structures
  • Prepping land for hunting, trails, or recreational use
  • Restoring longleaf pine ecosystems

How we approach the job

We flag mature trees you want preserved before we start. The mulcher head allows us to work right up to trunks without bark damage. Most underbrush jobs finish faster than full clearing because there's less material to process.

Frequently asked questions

Will you damage the trees you leave?

No. The mulcher head only contacts material we direct it at, and we hand-finish near trunks. Our equipment is far gentler on standing trees than dozing or skidding.

How is this different from bush hogging?

Bush hogs only cut what they can reach with their blades — they choke on anything woody over an inch or so, and the cuttings re-sprout fast. A forestry mulcher grinds material to fiber, top to bottom, even on standing palmetto and small trees.

Can you remove invasives like Chinese tallow?

Yes. Mulching is one of the most effective initial treatments for tallow, privet, and similar invasives, especially combined with a follow-up herbicide pass on the stumps.

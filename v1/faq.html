Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from Panhandle landowners about forestry mulching and land clearing.

What is forestry mulching?

Forestry mulching is a one-pass clearing method that uses a high-horsepower drum mulcher (mounted on a tracked carrier) to grind brush, palmetto, and small trees into mulch right where they stand. The material stays on site as a protective layer — no burning, no hauling.

How much does it cost?

Most Panhandle properties come in between $1,200 and $2,800 per acre, depending on density, terrain, and access. Light underbrush is the lower end; heavy palmetto and mixed tree growth is the higher end. We give fixed-price quotes in writing.

How is mulching different from bush hogging?

A bush hog cuts grass and light brush only — it chokes on anything woody. A forestry mulcher grinds standing brush, palmetto, and trees up to ~8" in diameter into fine mulch in a single pass.

Will mulching damage my soil or my trees?

No. Our tracked carriers spread weight far better than dozers, and the resulting mulch layer reduces erosion. We mark and preserve every tree you want to keep before any machine starts.

How long does it take?

Most jobs under five acres are finished in 1–2 days. Density and access matter — a thick palmetto five-acre tract can take longer than ten acres of light understory.

Do you handle stumps?

Yes, on request. For pasture or recreational use, leaving stumps under the mulch line is often fine. For building pads we grind or pull stumps as part of the scope.

Do you work in the rain or on wet ground?

We can work damp ground but not saturated ground — that's how you get ruts. After heavy rain we typically wait a few days before resuming work.

Are you licensed and insured?

Yes. We carry general liability and equipment insurance and can provide a certificate before work starts.

Where do you work?

The full Florida Panhandle — Pensacola to Tallahassee, north to the state line. See the service areas page for specifics.
